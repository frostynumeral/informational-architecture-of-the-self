\### Abstract  
For centuries, the ontology of human identity has oscillated between Cartesian substance dualism and mechanistic physicalist reductionism. Both paradigms falter when confronted with modern non-equilibrium thermodynamics, theoretical computer science, and quantum biology.

This paper develops a unified framework: the \*\*Informational Architecture of the Self (IAS)\*\*. We argue that the human self is neither an immutable, non-physical soul nor a static configuration of atomic particles. Instead, it is an autopoietic, computationally irreducible, and thermodynamically stabilized informational process.

By synthesizing Landauer’s principle of information erasure, England’s theory of dissipative adaptation, and Cronin and Walker’s Assembly Theory, we establish that biological individuality is an accumulated, physically recorded trajectory of causal memory operating far from thermodynamic equilibrium.

We examine the cognitive implementation of this architecture by evaluating Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR), demonstrating how intrinsic information (\$\\Phi\$) and quantum no-cloning constraints explain the unified, private nature of phenomenological experience without lapsing into ungrounded functionalism.

Finally, we address the metaphysical and legal consequences of this model: we utilize Stephen Wolfram’s computational irreducibility to construct a compatibilist framework for human agency, apply Derek Parfit’s Psychological Criterion ("Relation R") to whole-brain emulation, redefine mortality via information-theoretic death, and evaluate the emerging jurisprudence of digital personhood. We conclude that viewing the human as an informational entity does not reduce human agency to mere data processing, but grounds human dignity within the fundamental physics of a participatory cosmos.

\*\*Keywords:\*\* Informational Ontology, Landauer’s Principle, Assembly Theory, Integrated Information Theory (IIT), Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR), Computational Irreducibility, Relation R, Information-Theoretic Death, Substrate Independence.

\## 1. Introduction: The Informational Turn in Ontology and the Human Question

The question of what constitutes the human self remains the central problem of philosophical anthropology and the philosophy of mind. Historically, this inquiry has been dominated by two competing paradigms:

1\. \*\*Substance Dualism\*\*, which preserves human agency and subjective experience by positing an immaterial soul (\$res\\ cogitans\$) fundamentally detached from spatial, physical laws (\$res\\ extensa\$) \[1\].  
2\. \*\*Materialist Reductionism\*\*, which asserts that the self is an epiphenomenal byproduct of microphysical particles, reducing human experience and identity to the kinetic collisions of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms \[2\].

Both models face severe theoretical impasses. Dualism cannot explain how an immaterial entity exerts causal force on physical brain matter without violating the conservation of energy \[3\]. Conversely, crude materialist reductionism struggles with the \*\*Problem of Compositional Plasticity\*\*: biological organisms continuously turn over their material substrate through cellular respiration, mitosis, and metabolic exchange. An adult human replaces the vast majority of their physical atoms over a seven-to-ten-year cycle, yet personal identity and historical continuity persist \[4\]. If the physical matter is constantly replaced, the locus of selfhood cannot be the matter itself.

To resolve this impasse, contemporary natural philosophy has undertaken an \*\*Informational Turn\*\*. Concurrently, Luciano Floridi’s \*Informational Structural Realism\* (ISR) posits that physical objects are ultimately dynamic clusters of informational structures interacting within structural constraints \[8\].

\### 2.1. Landauer’s Principle and the Energetic Currency of Mind  
The physical nature of information is grounded in Landauer’s Principle \[10\]. Rolf Landauer demonstrated that while reversible computing can theoretically occur without minimal energy dissipation, any logically irreversible operation—specifically the erasure of a bit or the merging of distinct computational trajectories—demands a thermodynamic penalty:

\$\$\\Delta Q \\ge k T \\ln 2\$\$

Where \$k\$ is the Boltzmann constant and \$T\$ is the absolute temperature of the thermal reservoir. Erasing a single bit of information at room temperature (\$T = 300\\text{ K}\$) requires the dissipation of approximately:

\$\$W_{\\text{min}} \\approx 2.87 \\times 10^{-21}\\text{ Joules} \\ (\\approx 0.018\\text{ eV})\$\$

This thermodynamic limit bridges abstract computation and physical reality, resolving the paradox of Maxwell’s Demon \[11\]. The Demon can acquire information about molecular velocities to momentarily reduce system entropy, but to reset its physical memory registers to begin another cycle, it must erase its stored bits. This erasure expels heat into the environment, ensuring the Second Law of Thermodynamics (\$\\Delta S_{\\text{universe}} \\ge 0\$) remains unbroken \[12\].

For human identity, Landauer's Principle proves that \*\*cognition has a thermodynamic cost\*\*. The human brain, operating within an energetic budget of approximately \$12\\text{ to }20\\text{ Watts}\$, is an evolutionary masterpiece of near-Landauer efficiency \[13\]. It continuously processes sensory inputs, maintains homeostatic equilibrium, consolidates autobiographical memories, and clears transient neural states. The physical continuity of selfhood requires a continuous thermodynamic expenditure to actively clear entropic noise and preserve cohesive structural data.

\### 2.2. Dissipative Adaptation: Life as Non-Equilibrium Structural Entropy Export  
If the universe trends relentlessly toward maximum entropy (disorder), how does a structured, highly organized informational identity arise and persist over decades?

Jeremy England’s theory of \*\*dissipative adaptation\*\* provides the non-equilibrium statistical mechanics that answer this question \[14, 15\]. When an arbitrary, classical collection of interacting particles is driven by an external energy source (e.g., electromagnetic radiation, chemical fuel) while coupled to a heat bath, the system reorganizes its internal configuration to optimize the absorption and dissipation of that energy as heat:

\$\$P(I \\to F) / P(F \\to I) = \\exp\\left(\\frac{\\Delta Q_{\\text{diss}}}{k T}\\right)\$\$

Under driven conditions, configurations that are exceptionally adept at converting directed work into dissipated ambient heat become exponentially more probable over time \[14\].

Biological life, and by extension the human organism, is an advanced macroscopic realization of this physical law. The human body is a \*\*Non-Equilibrium Steady State (NESS)\*\* system. We are not static crystalline forms, but dynamic vortex-like structures that maintain an internal basin of low entropy (\$dS_{\\text{internal}} \\le 0\$) by consuming free energy, extracting relational data, and expelling high-entropy heat and waste into our surrounding environment (\$dS_{\\text{environment}} \\ge 0\$) \[16\].

The "self" is the ongoing informational topology of this dissipative vortex. The moment this continuous informational and energetic throughput ceases, the system relaxes to thermodynamic equilibrium: biological death.

\### 2.3. Assembly Theory and Causal Memory  
While dissipative adaptation explains the drive toward self-organization, it does not explicitly quantify the depth of historic information embodied within a specific physical object. \*\*Assembly Theory (AT)\*\*, developed by Cronin, Walker, et al., resolves this by defining an object not merely as an instantaneous arrangement of atoms, but by the size of its minimal historical formation pathway \[17, 18\].

The \*Assembly Index\* (\$A_X\$) of an object \$X\$ is defined as the minimal number of recursive steps required to construct \$X\$ from an initial pool of basic building blocks, using operations that can reuse previously generated substructures:

\$\$A_X = \\min_{\\Gamma} |\\Gamma|\$\$

Where \$\\Gamma\$ represents the directed acyclic graph of assembly operations. Cronin and Walker established a critical, empirically verified threshold using mass spectrometry and molecular fragmentation:

\$\$\\text{Threshold: } A_X \\ge 15\$\$

Molecules or macroscopic entities with an assembly index \$A_X \\ge 15\$ do not form spontaneously in detectable quantities via unguided, stochastic physical processes \[17\]. Their existence in high copy numbers (\$N \\gg 1\$) requires a historical mechanism of directed, evolutionary selection—in short, the presence of life or technology \[18\].

Assembly Theory changes how we conceptualize human identity. A human being is not an isolated, instantaneous time-slice of matter. A human being is an informational object with an astronomical assembly index, physically embodying a continuous, unbroken chain of causal memory spanning roughly 3.8 billion years of evolutionary selection, refined across decades of ontogenetic and neural development \[18\]. The physical matter of our bodies is simply the substrate upon which this immense chain of historical information is recursively written.

\### 3.2. Neo-Hylomorphism: Information as Substantial Form  
This physical-informational interplay provides an empirical grounding for a modernized, naturalized version of Aristotle’s \*\*Hylomorphism\*\* \[25\]. In classical hylomorphism, every concrete individual (\$substantia\$) is an inseparable compound of:

\* \*\*Matter\*\* (\*hyle\*): The underlying potentiality, the physical substrate.  
\* \*\*Form\*\* (\*morphe\*): The actualizing principle, the organizational pattern that makes a thing what it is \[26\].

For centuries, Cartesian dualism dismissed hylomorphism by degrading matter into inert, lifeless extension (\$res\\ extensa\$) and projecting "form" into an immaterial, transcendent realm. Conversely, reductionist materialism attempted to eliminate "form" entirely, asserting that only microscopic particles are real.

\*\*Neo-Hylomorphism\*\* reclaims the Aristotelian insight through the mathematics of non-linear dynamics and information theory \[27\]. In a human being:  
\* The \*matter\* is the transient, fluxional collection of carbon, nitrogen, calcium, and water atoms.  
\* The \*form\* is not a mystical soul, but the integrated, self-maintaining, and recursively operational \*\*informational architecture\*\* of the organism.

The causal power of the human organism cannot be understood by isolating its material parts; the system exhibits true \*\*downward causation\*\* (or contextual constraint) \[28\]. The global informational structure of the living network constrains, channels, and directs the microphysical behavior of its constituent atoms \[28\].

The informational self is the \*substantial form\* of the living body—an organization that actively transforms indeterminate chemical matter into a unified, functioning individual \[27\].

\### 4.1. Intrinsic Information: Integrated Information Theory (IIT)  
Classical computer science relies on \*\*Shannon Information\*\*, which quantifies the reduction of uncertainty across an observer-dependent communication channel \[30\]:

\$\$H(X) = -\\sum_{i=1}^{n} P(x_i) \\log_2 P(x_i)\$\$

Shannon’s formalism is strictly functional and extrinsic; it measures how much data an \*external receiver\* can extract from a message. It is indifferent to meaning, semantic value, or subjective experience \[30\].

To identify the cognitive locus of the self, we must move from extrinsic information to \*\*intrinsic information\*\*. This is the foundational insight of Giulio Tononi’s \*\*Integrated Information Theory (IIT)\*\* \[31, 32\]. IIT posits that consciousness is an intrinsic, fundamental property of physical systems that satisfy specific structural postulates:

1\. \*\*Existence:\*\* The system must have cause-effect power upon itself.  
2\. \*\*Composition:\*\* It must be composed of structured, elemental parts.  
3\. \*\*Information:\*\* It must specify a particular cause-effect state that differs from alternative possibilities.  
4\. \*\*Integration:\*\* Its cause-effect power must be irreducible to independent component subsystems (\$\\Phi > 0\$).  
5\. \*\*Exclusion:\*\* It must specify this cause-effect structure over a unique, maximal set of elements, defining a distinct phenomenological boundary (a Maximally Irreducible Conceptual Structure, or MICS).

The quantitative metric of this integrated information is \$\\Phi\$ (Phi) \[31\]. If a system has a high \$\\Phi\$, it possesses a vast, unified repertoire of self-referential cause-effect states.

The human cerebral cortex achieves an extraordinarily high \$\\Phi\$ because its structural topology combines localized functional specialization with extensive, global, reentrant connectivity \[32\]. Structures like the cerebellum contain billions more neurons than the cortex, but their feedforward, modular architecture yields a minimal \$\\Phi\$, explaining why cerebellar damage causes motor deficits without extinguishing conscious awareness \[31\].

Under IIT, the "self" at any given conscious moment is identical to the maximal conceptual structure specified by the brain’s integrated cause-effect network.

\### 4.2. Quantum Non-Cloning and Orch-OR: The Physical Privacy of Qualia

The \*\*Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR)\*\* model, formulated by Roger Penrose and Stuart Hameroff, introduces quantum mechanics to address this limitation \[33, 34\]. Orch-OR proposes that conscious moments arise from quantum computations orchestrated within the cytoskeletal \*\*microtubules\*\* of brain neurons \[34\].

These tubulin lattices sustain coherent quantum superposition states that grow until they reach the objective reduction threshold defined by Penrose's quantum-gravitational instability criterion:

\$\$E_G \\approx \\frac{\\hbar}{\\tau}\$\$

Where \$E_G\$ is the gravitational self-energy of the separated space-time geometries associated with the superposition, \$\\hbar\$ is the reduced Planck constant, and \$\\tau\$ is the coherence time until spontaneous state reduction \[33\]. Once collapsed, this objective reduction constitutes a non-computable, instantaneous moment of subjective experience (a conscious \*now\*) \[34\].

\### 4.3. The Symbol Grounding Problem and Autopoiesis  
An informational theory of the self must avoid the pitfall of reducing human beings to abstract, disembodied Turing machines. This limitation is exposed by Stevan Harnad’s \*\*Symbol Grounding Problem\*\* \[38\] and John Searle’s classic \*\*Chinese Room Argument\*\* \[39\]: formal, syntactic symbol manipulation alone can never produce semantic comprehension or intentionality.

Luciano Floridi expanded this into the \*Zero Semantic Commitment\* condition, demonstrating that an autonomous informational agent cannot derive intrinsic meaning from ungrounded data streams without recursive, homeostatic sensorimotor coupling with an external environment \[40\].

Human informational architecture is grounded because it is \*\*autopoietic\*\*—it is a physically embodied, self-producing entity \[41\]. As neurobiologists Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela demonstrated, an autopoietic system regenerates the very boundary conditions that sustain it \[41\].

The information processed by a human brain is not a detached sequence of binary bits; it is continuously grounded in the urgent, life-or-death imperatives of somatic survival: regulating blood glucose, maintaining body temperature, preserving structural boundaries, and evading physical harm \[42\].

Human identity is an \*\*embodied informational architecture\*\*. We do not "have" bodies that process data; our bodies are the thermodynamic grounding engines that make the informational data \*mean\* something to the system itself \[42\].

\### 5.1. Determinism and Computational Irreducibility: The Mechanics of Free Will  
One of the most persistent objections to naturalizing the self is that if the universe is fundamentally governed by deterministic physical laws, human agency and free will must be an illusion. If our brains are merely complex informational engines, our choices were predetermined at the Big Bang \[43\].

Stephen Wolfram’s principle of \*\*Computational Irreducibility\*\* resolves this tension without requiring supernatural intervention \[44\]. Wolfram demonstrates that for a vast range of deterministic systems—even simple, discrete cellular automata (e.g., Rule 30)—there is no mathematical shortcut to predict the system’s future states.

To determine the state of an irreducible system at step \$t_n\$, an observer cannot apply an algebraic formula; they must literally execute the computational steps of the system in real time:

\$\$\\text{Steps to determine state at } t_n \\approx n\$\$

The human brain, an extraordinarily complex, non-linear recurrent informational network operating at the edge of chaos, is computationally irreducible \[44, 45\].

This irreducibility provides a mathematically rigorous compatibilist foundation for human agency:

\* Even in a universe governed by deterministic underlying physical laws, the path of human decision-making cannot be compressed, predicted, or bypassed by an outside observer.  
\* The only way for the universe to know what a human being will decide in a complex moral dilemma is for that human being to run the physical computation through their neural architecture \[44\].

The subjective experience of deliberation—the feeling that the future is genuinely open—is not an illusion. It is the phenomenological expression of an agent executing a computationally irreducible program whose outcome remains unknowable until physically realized \[45\].

Imagine a device that reads an individual’s precise molecular and synaptic state on Earth, dissolves their biological matter into energy, and transmits the resulting data string via laser to Mars, where identical, local atoms are synthesized into an exact molecular copy \[46\].  
\* Under a naive materialist framework, the traveler was vaporized, and the person who wakes up on Mars is an identical imposter \[47\].  
\* Under Parfit’s \*\*Psychological Criterion\*\*, this intuition relies on an outmoded metaphysics. Parfit argued that what matters for human survival is not strict, numerical identity of physical matter, but \*\*Relation R\*\*: \*psychological connectedness and continuity\* maintained through an unbroken, functional causal chain \[46\].

For an informational self, identity is \*\*pattern-theoretic\*\*, not substance-dependent \[48\]. The continuity of the self does not require the persistence of the specific carbon atoms that inhabited the brain last week; it requires the preservation of the informational topology, synaptic weight matrices, experiential memory records, and executive personality traits that define the individual's mental life \[48\].

\### 5.3. Information-Theoretic Death  
This informational paradigm redefines human mortality. Historically, death has been defined by clinical thresholds:  
\* The cessation of cardiopulmonary circulation (clinical death).  
\* The irreversible loss of brainstem reflexes (whole-brain death) \[49\].

A human being is information-theoretically dead if and only if the physical structures that encode their autobiographical memory, learned skills, and personality traits have undergone such extreme entropic degradation that it is impossible—\*in principle\*, under any conceivable technology permitted by the laws of physics—to reconstruct the original informational state \[50\].

This legislative trend sets up a major jurisdictional conflict: if a human being slowly replaces damaged brain tissue with neuromorphic, synthetic implants, at what percentage of synthetic conversion do their constitutional rights disappear \[54\]?

\### 6.2. Neuro-Rights and the Commodification of the Self  
Simultaneously, international governance bodies are establishing new human rights frameworks to protect the informational self from corporate and state exploitation. In 2025, UNESCO adopted the \*Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology\*, formalizing the recognition of \*\*Neuro-Rights\*\* \[56\]. These rights include:

1\. \*\*Mental Privacy:\*\* Prohibiting the unconsented harvesting, reading, or monetization of neural decoding data.  
2\. \*\*Cognitive Liberty:\*\* Guaranteeing the individual's right to alter, augment, or maintain their cognitive states without state or corporate coercion.  
3\. \*\*Psychological Continuity:\*\* Protecting the integrity of an individual's autobiographical memory and behavioral traits against intrusive algorithmic manipulation.

By establishing that an individual's neural data \*is\* the core of their personal identity, international law is actively adopting the premise of this paper: \*\*a violation of an individual’s informational architecture is not a mere breach of property; it is a direct assault on the person itself\*\* \[56\].

\## 7. Conclusion: Toward an Integrated Informational Humanism

The statement that \*"We humans are just information in the Universe"\* should not be read as a nihilistic surrender to cold, mechanical abstraction. To be "information in the universe" is not to be a series of dry, corporate balance sheets or static strings of binary code on a hard drive.

Rather, as we have shown:  
1\. \*\*Thermodynamically\*\*, the human self is an extraordinary, non-equilibrium dissipative architecture—a localized, dynamic triumph over entropic decay that converts ambient energy into self-stabilizing causal memory, maintaining low entropy across decades \[10, 14, 18\].  
2\. \*\*Biologically\*\*, the human self is an evolving masterwork of code duality, balancing the long-term, error-correcting fidelity of digital memory with the dynamic, real-time adaptation of analog, somatic interaction \[19, 27\].  
3\. \*\*Cognitively\*\*, the self is a unified locus of intrinsic cause-effect power (\$\\Phi\$), anchored by an autopoietic biological body and protected by the fundamental privacy of quantum non-cloning constraints \[31, 34, 37\].  
4\. \*\*Metaphysically\*\*, the self is a computationally irreducible agent—one whose choices cannot be compressed or predicted by any entity prior to physical runtime, providing a rigorous physical foundation for human agency within a lawful cosmos \[44\].
